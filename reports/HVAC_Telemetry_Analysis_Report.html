HVAC IoT Telemetry Analysis

This analysis examines approximately two weeks of HVAC telemetry data across five devices. The analysis covers data cleaning, time-series summaries, anomaly detection, energy usage and operational insights.

Data Quality Summary

  • 32 duplicate rows were removed.
  • 25 implausible room-temperature readings were converted to missing values.
  • The data was aligned to a regular 15-minute interval.
  • 282 missing 15-minute intervals were created during alignment and flagged as gaps.
  • 136 offline/gap events were detected from the original telemetry intervals.

Key Findings

  • DVC-101 had the highest estimated energy consumption, at approximately 179.57 kWh.
  • DVC-104 had the highest setpoint miss rate, at approximately 25.3%, while the other devices were around 7–9%.
  • DVC-104 showed suspicious flatline temperature behaviour. Its room temperature remained around 23.5°C for an extended period, suggesting a possible stuck or faulty sensor.
  • DVC-101 recorded 15 high-power readings above 2000 W. Of these, 11 occurred while the compressor was OFF, making them important candidates for further equipment or telemetry investigation.
  • Power usage followed a clear daily cycle, with the main peak occurring around the morning operating period and lower usage overnight.

Business Recommendations

  • Investigate DVC-104 first because its temperature-control performance is substantially worse than the other devices and its temperature signal shows suspicious flatline behaviour.
  • Investigate the high-power readings from DVC-101, particularly cases where power remains very high while the compressor is reported OFF.
  • Prioritise monitoring of devices with repeated telemetry gaps, especially DVC-102, DVC-103 and DVC-104.
  • Use the observed daily power pattern to focus energy-efficiency checks and operational monitoring on higher-usage periods.

Assumptions and Limitations

  • A room-temperature deviation greater than 1°C from the setpoint was used as the working definition of a setpoint miss.
  • Power readings above 2000 W were treated as high-power spike candidates for investigation.
  • Energy consumption was estimated assuming each telemetry reading represents approximately 15 minutes.
  • Missing 15-minute intervals were treated as potential telemetry/offline gaps.
  • Repeated room-temperature values were treated as potential sensor issues when they showed unusually persistent behaviour.
